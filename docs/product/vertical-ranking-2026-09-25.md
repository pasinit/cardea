# Cardea: vertical selection without a personal network

Research date: 2026-09-25. Status: comparative desk research and proposed commercial tests. No outreach, buyer interviews, customer data access, price validation or paid pilots occurred in this study. No vertical or implementation dependency is selected.

## Recommendation

Investigate **B2B IT/software consultancies, accounting/outsourced finance firms, and cybersecurity/compliance consultancies** first. They combine recurring cross-functional work, identifiable business buyers and plausible repeatability. Test all three before committing to one. Their relative order is uncertain; the evidence supports a shortlist better than an exact winner.

The product remains a company operating layer. Specialize the first application, vocabulary, integrations and acquisition channel around a repeatable process. Start with one organization, two or three functions, at least two operational systems and a complete handoff/outcome. A smaller organization can meet that scope; company-wide usefulness does not require starting with a giant enterprise.

**Do not depend on the founder's personal relationships.** Public account discovery, a clear process-specific offer, demonstrable results and repeatable acquisition must carry the commercial case. Public listings establish discoverability, not access to data, meetings, purchase intent or permission to contact people indiscriminately.

## Scope and assumptions

This is a broad screen of 24 plausible segments or buying functions, not a claim to exhaust every industry. Some overlap: a manufacturing company may also buy a legal-operations application. They cannot be summed into a market-size estimate.

Assumptions: a small new vendor, no warm introductions, a goal of learning toward first repeatable revenue over roughly 6–12 months, English-language selling and a UK-first discovery surface. International software ecosystems inform the comparison; geography, budget and deadline are planning assumptions rather than user commitments. The shortlist targets organizations with distinct operations and finance responsibilities and an accountable technical operator; an initial 30–300-person search filter is a hypothesis, not a hard product limit. It broadens the earlier report's tentative 200–2,000-person company profile.

We considered professional services, commerce, physical operations, regulated services, nonprofits and public institutions. Consumer assistants, frontier-model training, autonomous clinical/legal/credit decisions and machinery control are outside this first company-process screen. A strong business in any of those areas would require a substantially different product evaluation.

M0–M5 provide a bounded execution foundation. None of these industry applications is available today. Every candidate still needs company enrollment, real connectors, source authority, a reusable application boundary and operational supervision. Current external OpenRouter model calls do not establish private inference; one Debian installation does not establish SaaS multi-tenancy, a managed fleet or high availability. Cardea's distribution license remains a separate unresolved decision.

## How the scores work

All scores are **analyst judgments on a 1–5 ordinal scale**, informed by the primary sources below. They are not measured profit margins, conversion rates, willingness to pay or forecasts. Higher is better.

| Dimension | What a high score means |
|---|---|
| V: value/budget potential | Frequent costly work with a plausible budget owner and a measurable outcome |
| R: repeatability | Similar integrations and process patterns could serve multiple customers |
| D: margin defensibility | Room to charge after competitive pressure, customization and ongoing support |
| B: buyer discoverability | Suitable organizations and buyer roles can be identified through public channels |
| S: purchase ease | A plausible path to a bounded purchase without extensive enterprise procurement |
| I: deployment ease | Relatively attainable authorized systems, test data and first-deployment requirements |

Profit potential **P = 0.40V + 0.35R + 0.25D**. This estimates potential Cardea economics after establishing repeatability; it is neither the customer's industry margin nor simply contract size. Integration/support costs inform R and D, but actual costs are unknown.

Cold-start access **A = 0.35B + 0.35S + 0.30I**. Discovery, buying and authorized delivery are distinct; a public directory alone cannot make A high.

Combined priority **C = 0.55P + 0.45A**. This modestly favors sustainable economics while reflecting the user's need to reach customers independently. Identical C scores sort by A, then name, solely for reproducibility. Treat adjacent positions as ties in practice. Raw inputs, separate axis ranks and sensitivity ranks are in the [machine-readable scorecard](vertical-ranking-2026-09-25.json).

### Sensitivity and limits

Changing the combined weighting to 70% profit / 30% access makes accounting first and technical consultancies second. At 30% profit / 70% access, technical consultancies remain first, followed by e-commerce and marketing agencies. Accounting moves to fifth and compliance consultancies to fourth. All three recommended discovery markets stay in the top five under both weightings.

Changing one underlying rating by one point can move C by 0.135–0.22, larger than many gaps in this table. The decimal places make calculation auditable; they do not imply precise knowledge. Scores should be replaced with observed acquisition cost, setup effort, recurring usage and contribution economics as evidence arrives.

## What we would sell, who buys, and how to reach them

Each workflow below is a proposed application, not an observed unserved need. Each cited vendor source documents a relevant ecosystem or competing product; it does not substantiate Cardea demand or a competitor deficiency. Vendor claims are not independent performance evaluations. “Adjacent” evidence means the source does not directly establish the whole proposed segment/workflow. Commercial validation is absent for every row.

### 1. B2B IT and software consultancies

**Buyer:** COO / managing director. **First process:** Sales → delivery → finance: signed scope to project readiness and invoice evidence.

**Cold route:** HubSpot solutions directory; public consultancy websites. Qualify technical implementation firms, excluding marketing-only agencies.

**Why it may fail:** Technical buyers can build internally; fragmented project rules can turn delivery into consulting.

Evidence scope: direct. Sources: [HubSpot public directory](https://ecosystem.hubspot.com/marketplace/solutions), [Productive competing operations suite](https://productive.io/).

### 2. Accounting and outsourced finance firms

**Buyer:** Managing partner / operations director. **First process:** Client services → accounting delivery → reviewer/finance: document collection, close readiness, reviewed deliverables and billing.

**Cold route:** Xero advisor directory; publicly advertised practice operations roles.

**Why it may fail:** Karbon already combines workflow and AI. Client data boundaries, API access and professional review add work.

Evidence scope: direct. Sources: [Xero advisor directory](https://www.xero.com/uk/advisors/find-advisors/), [Karbon AI](https://karbonhq.com/en-GB/feature/ai).

### 3. Cybersecurity and compliance consultancies

**Buyer:** Founder / head of delivery. **First process:** Account management → assessment delivery → reviewer: evidence requests, remediation handoffs and accepted report preparation.

**Cold route:** Vanta service-provider ecosystem and public consultancy sites. Listing does not confer partnership or customer access.

**Why it may fail:** Vanta and adjacent platforms already automate evidence. Need a demonstrated gap across systems, not another compliance chatbot.

Evidence scope: direct. Sources: [Vanta partner ecosystem](https://www.vanta.com/partners/partner-program).

### 4. Managed IT service providers: internal operations

**Buyer:** Owner / service delivery director. **First process:** Sales → service desk → finance: contract handoff, onboarding readiness, service evidence and billing reconciliation.

**Cold route:** Public MSP websites and vendor communities; qualify independently. No verified account list assembled.

**Why it may fail:** Rewst is a strong specialist. Restrict first scope to MSP internal operations; managing many customer environments needs additional isolation and fleet capabilities.

Evidence scope: direct. Sources: [Rewst platform](https://rewst.io/platform), [Rewst integrations](https://rewst.io/integrations).

### 5. E-commerce brands with internal operations teams

**Buyer:** COO / head of operations. **First process:** Customer support → warehouse → finance: returns exceptions and reconciled refund evidence.

**Cold route:** Public brand websites, Shopify ecosystem and operations communities; no marketplace acceptance assumed.

**Why it may fail:** Easy-to-reach does not mean profitable: Shopify Flow and abundant apps constrain pricing, while high-volume support can remain expensive.

Evidence scope: direct. Sources: [Shopify Flow workflow examples](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-flow/getting-started/workflow-examples).

### 6. Marketing and creative agencies

## The three discovery offers

These offers preserve the company-wide requirement. Functions may be small, but the application must cross their real responsibilities and systems.

| Candidate | Concrete offer to test | First integrations to investigate, not selected | Evidence that would justify Cardea |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical consultancies | Turn a signed scope into an accepted delivery handoff, authorized project setup and invoice-ready milestone evidence | CRM, project/issue tracker, accounting; narrow to two systems for the first complete process | Staff repeatedly reconcile ambiguous scope, delivery evidence and billing conditions across tools; a maintained workflow baseline cannot achieve the same quality/cost |
| Accounting / outsourced finance | Coordinate a recurring client close from missing inputs to reviewed deliverables and billing evidence | Practice management, accounting, document store; select the minimum pair from an observed process | Multiple functions lose measurable time to exceptions and handoffs that the existing practice suite cannot economically resolve |
| Compliance consultancies | Take a client evidence request through assessment, remediation handoff and a reviewed report package | Compliance platform, issue tracker, documents | Cross-client delivery is repetitive but evidence exceptions require judgment; Cardea can preserve client separation and reduce reviewer effort beyond existing platform configuration |

Technical consultancies have the best initial balance, but they can also build their own automation. An integration firm might become a channel later; that is not established by its public listing. Start with its own internal process before promising it a multi-customer managed platform.

Accounting has the strongest recurring-service economics in the shortlist, but Karbon and established accounting workflows set a demanding baseline. Compliance consultancies have technical buyers and explicit evidence needs, but existing compliance platforms already cover much of the job. None is an uncontested market.

Do not specialize the kernel around invoices, grants or matters. Reuse source-native records and maintained connectors; put domain schemas, process definitions and instructions in applications. A successor task must remain below its supervisor's live authority. Handoffs between separately authorized departments require separately admitted work, not pooled privileges.

## Reassessing law, large NGOs and private AI

**Law remains credible but is not the default first market.** The proposed entry is firm operations, not competing with legal-research assistants. Public firm lists help identify prospects, while matter confidentiality, incumbents and system access still make delivery harder. One verified, accessible repeated process could improve its ranking materially.

**Large NGOs are a plausible later segment or an evidence-driven exception.** Program, grants and finance coordination fits the product concept. NetHope provides a visible ecosystem, so the route need not depend on personal connections. However, neither mission importance nor grant volume proves an accessible internal automation budget. We need an accountable buyer, a funded operating problem and a feasible data/hosting boundary. The low cold-start ranking is about this new vendor's acquisition and delivery uncertainty, not NGO value or universal inability to pay.

**Private AI is a cross-sector requirement, not a market by itself.** Separate privately deployed software, organization-owned data/processes, private inference and proprietary model ownership. A customer can require control of its data while using maintained open-source software and open-weight or contracted models. Determine which boundary buyers will pay for before adding a local-model deployment profile. NGOs, law firms, healthcare providers and ordinary consultancies can all express that requirement. Proprietary foundation-model development is not justified by this research.

## Profitability: the measurement that should replace the scores

A large annual subscription can be a poor business if each customer requires bespoke integrations and continuous support. Track the full first-year contribution:

> Subscription + onboarding revenue − provider/hosting costs − support labor − implementation labor − acquisition costs.

Charge founder time at an explicit replacement-cost assumption. Also report recurring contribution separately from onboarding. These are contribution measures, not company net profit: shared engineering, administration, taxes, churn and renewal acquisition remain outside them.

An illustrative sensitivity calculation—not a price recommendation, sales forecast or sector benchmark:

| Same hypothetical contract, different delivery burden | Repeatable case | Bespoke case |
|---|---:|---:|
| Annual subscription | €24,000 | €24,000 |
| Onboarding revenue | €6,000 | €6,000 |
| Provider/hosting assumption | €2,400 | €2,400 |
| Support at €75/hour | 48 h = €3,600 | 192 h = €14,400 |
| Implementation at €600/day | 8 days = €4,800 | 25 days = €15,000 |
| Acquisition cash + priced labor | €4,000 | €4,000 |
| First-year contribution before shared overhead | **€15,200 (50.7% of revenue)** | **−€5,800 (−19.3%)** |

No actual Cardea deal has these economics. The purpose is to expose the variables that matter. First-year positive contribution is still insufficient without viable retention, support obligations and enough contribution to fund ongoing engineering. Record elapsed time to cash and payment terms as well as nominal contract value.

## Validation without personal introductions

Proposed first experiment: three matched account cohorts, one per recommended segment. This is a future execution protocol; no messages have been sent and no lead list is claimed as complete.

1. **Qualify 30 organizations per segment from public sources.** Record the company URL, relevant functions, plausible buyer role, evidence of the repeated service/process and any visible stack information. Distinguish evidence from guesses. Exclude solo practices, organizations without the required cross-team scope and firms whose stated need requires unsupported fleet or multi-tenant operation. Do not assume a directory entry is current, contactable or qualified.
2. **Prepare one process-specific demonstration and one-page offer per segment.** Show synthetic inputs, an exception, a cross-team handoff, source permissions, a recovery case and the final acceptance evidence. Label every synthetic result. Sell a bounded operational outcome; avoid requiring buyers to understand an “agent OS.” Do not build three complete products before discovery.
3. **Test a repeatable cold channel when outreach is separately instructed.** Use appropriate public business channels and targeted, relevant messages. Ask about the existing process and its costs before pitching private AI. Partner directories are discovery tools; partnership, marketplace listing and referral access are separate applications, not assumed distribution.
4. **Seek five substantive conversations and two process walkthroughs per segment.** These are planning targets, not a sample-size proof. Include the operator, budget owner and a data/IT owner across discovery; five enthusiastic developers without buying authority do not validate a market. A walkthrough should trace actual cases, systems, exceptions and current controls with permission.
5. **Propose a bounded paid pilot only after confirming a real gap.** State systems, outcome, review rubric, access, installation/support owner, implementation ceiling and price. A payment or signed scoped commitment is stronger evidence than general enthusiasm; it still does not establish retention.
6. **Compare cohorts and choose one application.** Track qualified accounts → reachable accounts → replies → substantive conversations → walkthroughs → funded commitments, plus cash and labor at every step. Separate deliverability and message failures from lack of demand. A nonresponse by itself does not disprove the workflow problem.

Suggested initial stop/revise gates: stop a segment if prospects consistently report that their existing product solves the job adequately; do not build where nobody can authorize the required systems; revise delivery if the first scoped offer cannot cover implementation/support; and reject an application that needs domain-specific kernel authority bypasses. Before selecting the winner, require a complete M6 job contract, baseline and a plausible positive contribution case supported by actual quotes and effort estimates. Prefer repeated evidence from multiple organizations over one unusually cooperative prospect.

After first recurring use, the second deployment must demonstrate reuse. Measure connector changes, custom business rules, onboarding time and support hours. If they do not fall as expected, price Cardea honestly as a services-intensive offering or change the application; do not call it scalable software on the basis of subscription billing alone.

## Implications for the roadmap and standards-first policy

This study informs M6; it does not complete discovery or select a vertical. M7–M10 remain contingent on the existing [validation protocol](validation.md). Update scores after real observations and preserve the dated baseline so later decisions are auditable.

For the selected workflow, compare the current process, its incumbent vertical suite, an adequate maintained open-source workflow/agent implementation and Cardea's smallest necessary integration. Candidates from the [existing market review](market-analysis-2026-09-25.md) and [ADR-020](../decisions/020-company-product-direction.md) remain candidates. Check current license, maintenance, edition/API rights and complete-process fit before selection. Marketing feature overlap is a reason to test, not evidence that an alternative fails. No dependency, custom protocol or new execution engine is authorized or selected by this ranking.

A stronger independent route to market is the immediate gap to investigate. The next product commitment should follow qualified discovery and a measurable cross-team job, not an assumed warm introduction, the largest possible contract, or an industry label alone.
